Scene 3

The premise

You're a dancer trapped in a 1930s speakeasy, and the Director calls you to the stage three times. Between shows, eight tables murmur in Orrin, a language that doesn't exist. Subtitles help, in English or Spanish.

Only one table says the briefcase code, and it changes every game. Earn $500 to bribe the guard, find the second half of the giant zipper in the cans, and escape before the clock runs out: 5, 7 or 12 minutes.

The experiment is in the ear: here information isn't handed over. You listen, compare and deduce.

8Tables
3Attempts
3Shows
The audio is both ambience and clue. An invented language means nobody “understands” the scene at first: every player starts equally lost.

Bar floor plan, 8 tables

Our reinterpretation

For us, Director's Cut meant re-reading the character. We took out the comedy: we believe this character was trying to escape a life of abuse and reach freedom, and that is why they run. In our cut, the escape is not a gag. It is the whole point.

Note on the character: the character we used is free of copyright.

Reflexes · Physics

The Falling Glass

Clean tables to earn cash, but watch the edge: when a glass slips, catch it mid-air in the sweet spot for a $50 tip—or pay for the broken glass.

Rhythm · 120–216 BPM

One-Hand Dance Show

Three mandatory stage calls that accelerate (1.0x, 1.5x, 1.8x). Simplified to four one-hand arrow keys after playtesting showed chords broke the flow.

Economy · Access

The $500 Bribe

Hustle tips across the bar to pay off the VIP bouncer guarding the office—unlocking access to the 4-digit briefcase.

Gesture · Audio

The Can Shelf

Shake cans to tell solid clues from hollow decoys by weight and sound, then trace a fast circle under 2 seconds to pop the lid.

1. Fast Discard in HTML

We tested coat-searching and darts in HTML, saw they slowed pacing, and cut them before wasting a single hour of 3D modeling.

2. Living .md + GDD Spec

Every validated variable (gravity 408 px/s², 110u audio radius, exact BP_Can & M_Can asset names) went into a shared Markdown spec.

3. Claude MCP → Unreal 5

With dev environments ready, Claude MCP migrated the logic into Blueprints & C++ in phases, while Margariza and Ceci polished the final Unreal details.

Deleted scenes

Pre-jam dry runs taught us to prepare our MCP and dev environments beforehand. Feeding Claude our living .md spec plus validated HTML code let us migrate mechanics with exact Unreal object names (BP_Can, M_Can, WBP_DanceModal) instead of starting from scratch.
Scene 5

From HTML to Unreal

Once a mechanic felt right in HTML, we wrote it into the spec and handed it to Claude MCP in phases. The spec also listed what already existed in Unreal (M_Can, BP_Can, tapa.png) and marked it off-limits, so nothing that worked got rebuilt.

Match length

5 · 7 · 12 min

Three modes, set from the GameMode. Always three dance shows, evenly spaced.

Rising tension

1.0× · 1.5× · 1.8×

Each show speeds up the music and the notes: 120, 180 and 216 BPM.

Late to the stage

2 strikes

Ignore the Director's call and you are dragged on stage. Twice, and it's game over.
